About Me

I am a first-year Ph.D. student at the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences (ICT, CAS) and the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences (UCAS), advised by Prof. Jian Wang. I am with the Microprocessor Research Center and the State Key Lab of Processors, ICT, CAS.

I received my B.Eng. degree in Electronic Information Engineering from ShanghaiTech University. My research interests lie in chiplet/D2D interconnects, memory-interconnect architecture, and workload-driven performance modeling for heterogeneous computing systems.

I am particularly interested in how realistic link and memory-system mechanisms, including packetization, credit-based flow control, queues, retry/replay, FIFO/CDC effects, and link latency/bandwidth constraints, shape workload-visible throughput, tail latency, and resource utilization. My background in high-speed wired circuits helps me connect physical-link constraints with architecture-level design choices.

Research Interests

  • Chiplet and D2D Interconnects: link architecture, flow control, packetization, arbitration, reliability mechanisms, and PHY-calibrated latency/bandwidth constraints.
  • Memory-Interconnect Architecture: memory hierarchy, on-chip and package-level fabrics, CXL/UCIe-style transaction semantics, and scalable data movement in heterogeneous systems.
  • Workload-Driven Performance Modeling: trace mapping, traffic characterization, event/cycle-level modeling, sensitivity analysis, and bottleneck attribution.
  • Data-Movement Bottleneck Analysis: effective bandwidth, average and tail latency, queue occupancy, credit stalls, replay overhead, and link utilization under realistic traffic patterns.
  • Hardware-Aware System Design: bridging circuit/link constraints and computer architecture decisions for processor, accelerator, and heterogeneous SoC systems.

Current Focus

My current focus is D2D interconnect mechanism-level performance modeling and workload-driven link architecture optimization for chiplet systems. I am interested in mapping traffic patterns such as bulk transfers, small random requests, bursty streams, dependent requests, and read/write-asymmetric workloads onto practical link mechanisms, and using the resulting bottlenecks to guide architecture exploration.
