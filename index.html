About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science and Technology at the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, advised by Prof. Jian Wang. I am with the Microprocessor Research Center and the State Key Lab of Processors, ICT, CAS.

I received my B.Eng. degree in Electronic Information Engineering from ShanghaiTech University. My current research interest is in data-movement bottleneck analysis for processor and heterogeneous SoC systems, with a focus on workload/trace-driven performance modeling, memory hierarchy, on-chip fabrics, and die-to-die interconnects.

I am particularly interested in how realistic interconnect and memory-system mechanisms, such as packetization, flow control, queues, retry/replay, FIFO/CDC effects, and link latency/bandwidth constraints, shape workload-visible throughput, tail latency, and resource utilization. My background in high-speed wired circuits helps me reason about physical-link constraints as part of architecture exploration rather than as an isolated circuit problem.

Research Interests

  • Data-Movement Bottleneck Analysis: workload traces, memory-access behavior, read/write traffic, burstiness, request dependency, and bottleneck attribution in processor and heterogeneous SoC systems.
  • Memory and Interconnect Architecture: memory hierarchy, NoC/SoC fabrics, die-to-die/chiplet interconnects, and CXL/UCIe-style transaction semantics as mechanisms for scalable data movement.
  • Performance Modeling and Architecture Exploration: event/cycle-level modeling, sensitivity analysis, effective bandwidth, average and tail latency, queue occupancy, credit stalls, and link utilization.
  • Hardware-Aware System Design: link-controller datapaths, packetization, FIFO/CDC behavior, retry/replay mechanisms, and PHY-calibrated latency/bandwidth/reliability constraints.

Current Focus

I am currently building a workload-driven methodology for understanding why practical memory and interconnect systems often fall short of ideal latency-bandwidth models. My near-term work uses D2D/chiplet links as a concrete setting to study how packetization, finite queues, credit-based flow control, retry/replay, and link-controller datapaths affect effective bandwidth, tail latency, and architecture design choices.
